Sending

Cast to anything that speaks UPnP

Any DLNA renderer on your network shows up in the picker: a network streamer, a smart TV, a wireless speaker. Where the renderer accepts the file as it is, nothing gets re-encoded on the way out. Where it does not, the app negotiates a format it can play, and tells you which of the two happened.

Receiving

Four ways to play into it

Receiver mode is not one door but four, each one switchable on its own: a DLNA renderer for any UPnP controller, an FCast receiver, an MPD server, and a classic network player. Whatever already controls your library can play into the phone, with your DAC on the end of it.

DLNA MediaRenderer FCast MPD server Classic network player
Volume

In bit-perfect mode, nothing touches the level

There is no software gain stage in the bit-perfect path, so an incoming volume command is declined rather than applied. Refusing to attenuate is the only way to stay bit-exact, and the app would rather report full volume than quietly throw bits away.

Honesty

It tells you when it had to convert

The badge reads either "Sent unchanged, receiver supports this format" or the exact conversion it had to make. A claim of untouched audio is worth nothing unless the app also admits the times it could not manage it.
