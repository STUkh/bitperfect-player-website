Direct USB DAC output

Android routes every stream through a system mixer that forces 48 kHz and truncates to 16-bit. Bitperfect opens the USB audio device itself, in exclusive mode, and locks your DAC to the file's own clock.

That reading is for a connection the app actually owns. Where an exclusive lock is not achievable, the badge says what is really happening instead of claiming one it does not have.

Exclusive mode

Your DAC answers to one app at a time

Bitperfect takes the USB audio device in exclusive mode, so a notification chime cannot duck your track, and the OS cannot quietly re-open the stream at its own preferred rate halfway through an album.

Rate switching

The clock follows the file, not the phone

A 44.1 kHz track followed by a 192 kHz one switches the hardware rate between them rather than resampling either to meet in the middle. Native DSD and DoP are both handled, up to DSD512.

PCM up to 768 kHz
Bit depth 16, 24 and 32
DSD DoP and native
Hardware

Dongles and desktop DACs, same path

Any USB-C or Micro-USB OTG DAC that speaks UAC1 or UAC2 works, from a pocket dongle to a desk unit. Where the DAC has its own hardware volume control, Bitperfect drives that control directly rather than attenuating in software and throwing bits away.

Honesty

The badge tells the truth, always

Bit-perfect is reserved for an unaltered stream to an external DAC. Turn on the EQ or the AI Enhancer and it changes to Enhanced. Fall back to the internal output and it says so. Five states, each meaning exactly one thing.

DSD

Two ways to carry a DSD stream, and it picks the right one

DSD64 through DSD512, sent natively where the DAC accepts it, or as DoP where it does not. The choice is made from what the device reports, not from a setting you have to understand, and neither path ever converts the stream down to ordinary PCM.

Range DSD64 to DSD512
Transport Native or DoP
