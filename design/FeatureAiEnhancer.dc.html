AI Enhancer

The enhancer reconstructs the upper band, roughly 14 to 22 kHz, on 44.1 and 48 kHz material, entirely on your phone's own neural chip.

Reconstructs 14 to 22 kHz
Works on 44.1 and 48 kHz
Runs on Neural chip
Works offline Yes
What it does

In-band reconstruction, not invention

It rebuilds amplitude and phase inside the band the file already occupies, around 14 to 22 kHz, at the same sample rate. It does not synthesise ultrasonic content that was never recorded, and it will not turn a 128 kbps MP3 into a master.

Band 14 to 22 kHz
Rates 44.1 and 48 kHz
Where it runs

On your phone, or not at all

It runs entirely on your phone's own neural chip. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is asked of the network while it plays. The model downloads once, the first time you switch the feature on, and after that it works with the radio off.

Hardware

No fallback, and that is deliberate

There are two settings: off, and the neural chip. There is no fallback path and no automatic guessing. On a phone without the hardware the feature is simply not offered, which is a better answer than one that stutters.

Off Neural chip (NPU)
Honesty

The badge stops saying bit-perfect

The moment the enhancer touches a sample, the signal indicator changes to Enhanced. It is DSP, it is doing something, and the app says so rather than letting a purity claim stand that is no longer true. Turning it on also switches the whole effects rack off: only one of the two can run at a time, and every affected row tells you why it went quiet.

Unavailable while AI Enhance is on: the two do not stack, so switching one on switches the other off.
